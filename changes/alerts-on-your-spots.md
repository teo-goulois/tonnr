## Alerts on your spots

A spot of your own now has an "Alerts" tab. Say in one sentence what makes the spot work: the swell's height, period and direction, the wind, the tide, and the shortest window worth your while. Each part of the sentence opens what changes it, and the days ahead show at once what your conditions give. What you ask of the tide is set on the tide's own curve, which shows the heights you take and where the tide is in them. An alert can cover neighbouring spots that work in the same conditions.

A spot can have several alerts, each with its name and its conditions: one for small clean days, one for big ones. The tab lists them with what each asks and its switch. Open one to change it, or start another from a copy of it.

You are told when a window takes shape on two forecasts in a row, again when one you were told of moves by more than two hours or goes, the evening before for all the spots of the next day, and when a window begins, with a recent reading of the waves at a station nearby, when there is one. What you were told is under the bell, with your alerts and your settings: how many days ahead, by daylight only, at the hours you give for each day of the week, and the date you are away until.

Say yes to mail with your first alert, or in the settings, and you get the same by mail at your account's address, once it is checked: never during your quiet hours, eight a day at most, and each mail stops them in one press. A mail that waited for the morning says where the window is then, in your units.
