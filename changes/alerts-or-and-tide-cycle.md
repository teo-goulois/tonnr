## An alert says "or", and asks the tide by its cycle

Each part of an alert can now take other values: waves of 1 to 2 m or of 3 m and more, a wind from the east or from the south. A "+" appears after a value once it is chosen, and the alert finds an hour that any of the values meets.

The tide is asked by the parts of its cycle, from low water through high water and back, "from mid tide rising to high", and by its coefficient, under 70, from 70 to 90 or over 90, on the coasts whose tables print one. A height is another stage on a day of big tides; a part of the cycle is the same whatever the tide. Alerts that asked a height keep it until the cycle is chosen in its place.

A new alert is written one part at a time, each chosen or passed. A part opens in a drawer on a phone and in a popover on a computer, with its value written big, the count of the week's windows as it changes, and two buttons. The sentence ends on "and it lasts at least", and says so when the tide asked lasts less than that. Under it, a line counts the windows of the week, and opens on the seven days and their hours.
