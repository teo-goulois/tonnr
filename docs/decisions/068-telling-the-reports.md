# 068. Telling the reports at the breaks an account keeps, and what a report's author reads of it

Status: accepted on 2026-10-19. Téo asked for it the day reports went online ([decision 067](067-reports-of-the-waves.md)): notifications "quand un report apparait sur un spot dans un favoris ou une liste et tout ce qui suit". He answered the ten questions I asked, each as I proposed, and added two things: the author of a report reads "des stats sur le nb de vue etc.", and the operators know "dans posthog etc." whether the feature is used. What is marked "chosen here" is his to overturn. Codex challenged the design before it was built; what it changed is said where it applies.

## Context

A report says what a break looks like now, and is worth a few hours. Whoever keeps that break in a list wants to know when one comes, while it is still true, without opening the app to look. The instance already tells an account by push and in the web app's list, for its alerts ([decision 031](031-alerts.md)), with a machinery built for windows of the forecast that move, merge and go: what was told at each spot, claims, a mail and a push that leave apart. A report is simpler: it comes once, at one break, and is true or gone.

## Decision

### Who is told, and of what

- An account is told of a report at a break when it keeps the break: in one of its lists, the favorites among them; in a list shared with it that it kept ([decision 035](035-shared-lists.md)); as the break one of its own spots was made from; or as the break of a spot of the owner's in a list shared with it, which Codex added. Never of its own report, and nothing while it is suspended or away ("away until" of its alert settings). Chosen here, at Téo's word.
- A switch of the account, "reports at my spots", on unless it is switched off, beside the moments of its alerts (`alert_settings.on_reports`). And a switch on each list, on unless switched off: the owner's on its list (`station_list.reports_on`), a member's on its membership (`station_list_member.reports_on`), neither changing the other's. A break is told when one of the lists that hold it says yes, or when it is the break of a spot of the account's own, which only the account's switch stops. Chosen here.
- A report is told when it is shown: its checks passed, or an operator showed it. Once: `surf_report.announced_at` says it was, whatever becomes of it later. It is told when it was first shown within two hours of being sent, and while it is less than three hours old: `surf_report.published_at` keeps the first moment it was shown, which an operator who shows it again does not change. Codex found that the moment the worker comes upon it would refuse a report shown at an hour and fifty-nine minutes and found at two hours and one. A report shown before this version has no such moment, and is not told. Chosen here.

### The notice

- What an account is told is a notice: an account, a break, the reports it holds (`report_notice_report`), when it began, whether it was read. A report that comes at a break within three hours of the beginning of the account's notice there joins it, and makes it unread again: an account has one notice at a break in three hours, whatever the number of reports. Chosen here.
- The notices are listed with what the alerts told, in the alerts' panel, by time, and counted in the same unread badge, through `reportNotices`: a feed of their own, so that `notifications` and the assistant's tool that reads it, `list_my_alert_notifications`, keep their answers. Codex asked for it. A notice says the break, how many reports, and the author, size, note and first photo of the newest one still shown. One whose reports are all hidden, removed or deleted says that the report was taken down, with nothing of it, and one whose reports are older than a week, which nobody reads any more, says that it is no longer shown.
- Pressed, in the list or as a push, a notice opens the break on its reports, `?break=<id>&tab=reports`, over whatever tab the panel kept, and is read: the panel shows every report there, those that joined it since too, so a notice pressed in a list loaded before one joined is read all the same. A push names its notice, `&notice=<id>`, which the app reads once the break is shown, for the account it was for and for no other, and keeps in the address until an account that is not signed in yet signs in. Codex found the three.
- The list loads the notices thirty at a time, the one a report joined last first, with a button for the older ones: the badge counts every unread one, and each can be reached. What the alerts told and the notices are kept in the browser under the account they were asked for: a tab whose session became another account's, in another tab, never shows the first one's. Codex found both.
- A notice is kept 30 days, then deleted. Chosen here.

### Push

- A notice is pushed to every browser where the account switched push on, as an alert is: one push for a notice, so one at most at a break in three hours. A report that joins a notice whose push has left adds no push; one that joins a push that waits is said in it. No mail: a mail is heavy for what is true for two hours. Chosen here, at Téo's word.
- It leaves at once, or at the end of the account's quiet hours, which a change of them brings forward, and is given up once the newest of its reports that is still shown is three hours old: a newer report that joined it and was hidden since does not keep it worth sending. It is written as it leaves, from the reports still shown then: none left, it does not leave.
- Six report pushes a day at most for an account, counted apart from the 24 of its alerts, by the day of UTC as theirs are, as each is handed over: one written anew between its two steps is counted once. One over the count is held, for good: the list still has it. Chosen here, at Téo's word.
- It says, in the account's language: "New report at <break>" or "3 reports at <break>", then the newest report's author, size and the start of its note. It shows the newest report's first photo where the browser shows a picture in a notification: Chrome on Android and on a computer does, Safari, the iPhone's among them, and Firefox do not. The picture is the address of its thumbnail, signed as the push is handed over for as long as the push is kept and an hour, and it goes with the words while both fit in one push, under the 3,993 bytes it holds: the words alone otherwise. Codex found that the push left everything but its four fields behind. Its tag is the break, so that a newer notice at a break takes the older one's place.
- It leaves in two steps, as an alert's push does. The first, holding the account, its settings and the notice, decides what it says and writes that it is leaving. The second holds the account, its settings, the notice, the reports it speaks of and the account's browsers, in that order, which is the API's; reads again whether the account still keeps the break, wants the reports and is there, and whether the break and the reports it was written from are the ones of the notice still, writing it anew otherwise, as when a merge moved the notice to another break; counts it; and hands it over. A flag, an operator's decision or a deletion of one of those reports waits for it, as does an account that switches the reports off or goes away, and none leaves once they are answered. A list changed in that moment does not stop it. A worker that stops between the two leaves it leaving, which is taken as told ten minutes later: a push is sent once at most. A push service that took it, or did not answer, has told it; every one saying no, it failed; every browser gone, it is given up, and the browsers are forgotten.
- A push that was taken cannot be called back. A report hidden after its push left stays on the screens it reached, and its picture's address works until it runs out, a few hours later: whoever holds it reads the thumbnail, as anyone who reads the report's tab while it is shown. The lock screen shows what the browser shows of a notification.

### What tells a report, and in what order

- The worker's job `tell-reports`, every minute on an instance that keeps media, tells what was shown since, then hands over what is due, then deletes the notices of more than thirty days. Telling a report is one transaction: a worker that stops tells none of it, and the next run tells it whole.
- It holds, in this order: the catalogue, beside others that only read it (`readCatalogue`), so that no merge moves a break under it; the break, so that two reports at it are told one after the other and the second finds the notice the first began; then the report. It writes rows that name accounts and holds none: a flag, a suspension or a deletion does not wait for it. A merge of two breaks moves the notices, then the reports, of the one that goes to the one that stays, in the order a push holds them: the reports were deleted with their break before, which Codex found.

### What the author reads

- The author of a report reads, under it, how many saw it, how many opened one of its photos or videos, and how many accounts it was told to, as it was told. An operator reads the same in the admin. Nobody reads who. The counts are in no answer to anyone else, and in no public page. Chosen here.
- A browser counts a report once, when its card has been half in view a second without a pause: in the panel of a break, or on the public page of the break. It keeps the reports it counted, a week, in its own storage, and tells the API twenty at a time, which keeps a number and nothing of who. A photo opened in the viewer is counted the same way, once a browser. The author's own account does not count; a key does not, a program has no screen. A caller, an account, a link or a visitor by a hash of its address, tells 120 times an hour at most, and nothing past them: the counts are a pleasure for the author, which anyone could raise a little, and bound what that costs. Codex asked for the bound.
- The number of accounts told is exact. Codex said that a small number tells how many follow a break, and asked to round it: it says how many, never who, of a break whose page anyone reads. Chosen here.

### What the operators read

- PostHog receives, from the API as every action does, with no report, break or list named: `reports_seen`, a browser's batch of views; `report_media_opened`; `report_notice_read`, which says a notice was opened, from the list or its push; `list_reports_switched`; and `alert_settings_updated`, which a change of the account's switch is. The worker sends nothing to PostHog, as before.
- The admin's overview says the week's reports, each by when it happened, as the rows keep them now: sent, first shown, refused by their checks, the notices made, the pushes that left and the notices read that week, and the views and opened photos of the week's reports; and, as things are now, the reports waiting for an operator, those the checks were unsure of and those the flags hid, and the accounts that switched notices off. Each with its own moment, which Codex asked for.

## Rejected

- **A notice for each report.** At a break that works on a Sunday morning, ten reports would be ten pushes. One notice in three hours says the same, and a report that joins it shows in the list.
- **A mail.** Heavy, late, and counted against the instance's daily mails, for what is true two hours.
- **The alerts' notifications and deliveries.** They keep, at each spot, what each way out told of moving windows: a report has nothing that moves, and its rules would have been a branch in each of theirs. Their feed is also an assistant's tool, whose answer would have changed.
- **Counting viewers by who they are**, an account's id or a hash of an address kept with the report. It would tell who looks at which break, for a figure that is a pleasure and no more.

## Consequences

- The migration `report-notices` adds the switches, `published_at`, `announced_at` and the counts to `surf_report`, and `report_notice` with `report_notice_report`. It marks every report already decided as told. Either program may be deployed first: a report that an earlier version showed has no `published_at`, and is not told.
- The worker sends report pushes with the pair of keys of its alerts' pushes: an instance whose worker has none lists the notices and pushes nothing.
- `/privacy` says who is told, that a notice is kept 30 days, what a push of a report carries, and that a report's views are counted with nothing of who while the browser keeps which reports it counted.
